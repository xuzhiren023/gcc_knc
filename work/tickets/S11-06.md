Lead observations to test, not conclusions:
1. **Our absolute numbers are low against known results.** Tuned STREAM triad on a 7120P is 175-192 GB/s
   (Intel, S11-05 [5]), and S8's write-only NGO test reached 226 GB/s; here triad tops out at 144, write at 125
   and read-only at 108. The whole sweep took 9 minutes against the 57 planned, so samples were short (default
   256 MiB per array, 8 passes: about 20 ms per read sample). Find out whether the benchmark itself under-reports:
   sample length (try `--mib 2048 --passes 32`), per-pass barrier and fence cost, thread start skew, the read
   kernel's instruction count and dependency chain (S11-05 documented write/triad instructions per line, not
   read), loop overhead, and whether the timed region includes anything but the passes. Calibrate against a
   STREAM-equivalent configuration that should reproduce 175+ GB/s triad; if it does not, that gap is the first
   thing to close.
2. One thread per core wins everywhere (60 threads beat 120 and 240 by 1.5-2x), and `spread` placement is
   about 2x `compact`/`reverse` at 60 threads. Is 60 threads with 1 per core enough outstanding misses, or do 2
   threads per core with deeper prefetch do better once samples are long?
3. Prefetch pf0 8 / pf1 64 helped copy and triad; eviction (l2 or both) lifted rmw from about 103 to 150; cyclic
   1024-line chunks lifted rmw to 128. Huge pages helped copy and triad little at 60 threads. Offsets moved
   triad within noise. The spreads (min to max) are wide in many cases, so treat single-case differences under
   10% as noise until repeated with longer samples.
