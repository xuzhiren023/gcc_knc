Facts already settled (do not re-derive):
- ADR-0003 point 4: vector division and square root are correctly rounded by default. They are
  built from `vrcp23ps`, `vrsqrt23ps`, FMA and fixups, and handle zero, infinity, NaN and
  subnormals. Until a sequence is proven for a mode, that mode stays scalar (x87). Approximate
  forms are opt-in only.
- IMCI has no vector divide or square root, and no FP64 estimate instruction.
- Startup MXCSR on the card is `0x00200000` (no FTZ, no DAZ). Packed add, sub and mul keep
  subnormals exactly, as x87 does (S3 card check, `fp_special.c`).
- The KNC FMA forms are `vfmadd132ps`/`213`/`231`, `vfmsub*`, `vfnmadd*` and `vfnmsub*`, plus the
  pd forms. Each takes a per-instruction rounding override (`{rn}`, `{rd}`, `{ru}`, `{rz}`) and
  `{sae}`.

Answer:

1. **Estimates.**
   - For `vrcp23ps` and `vrsqrt23ps` (and `vlog2ps`/`vexp223ps` if relevant), give from the manual:
     - the exact error bound and the input domain;
     - the behaviour on zero, infinity, NaN, negatives, and subnormal inputs and outputs;
     - the flags raised.
   - Any other IMCI aid: `vfixupnanps`, `vgetexpps`, `vgetmantps`, `vscaleps`, `vrndfxpntps`,
     and the rounding-mode overrides.
2. **FP32 division and square root.**
   - A sequence whose final result is correctly rounded in round-to-nearest, and under the other
     rounding modes if the sequence reads MXCSR.
   - A proof outline. Sources: Markstein 2000, "IA-64 and Elementary Functions"; Cornea et al.;
     Intel's published Itanium and KNC sequences; or Harrison's formal proofs.
   - The instruction count and the dependency chain.
   - How zero, infinity, NaN, overflow, underflow, and subnormal operands and results are handled:
     scaling with `vgetexp`/`vscale`, or a fixup path.
   - Which flags (`inexact`, `divide-by-zero`, `invalid`, `underflow`, `overflow`) the sequence
     raises, compared with x87 `fdiv` and `fsqrt` on the same values. Say which differences a C
     program could observe, and whether ADR-0003 must say so.
3. **FP64 division and square root.**
   - The same, starting either from an FP32 estimate of the converted operand (`vcvtpd2ps` then
     `vrcp23ps`) or from a table.
   - The instruction count.
   - Say plainly if a correct FP64 sequence is likely slower than x87 `fdiv` at 8 lanes. Then it
     should stay scalar, and that answers ADR-0003's open question.
4. **GCC 16 entry points.**
   - The `div<mode>3` and `sqrt<mode>2` expanders.
   - How i386 expands its own reciprocal sequences today, and why those are not correctly rounded:
     `ix86_emit_swdivsf`, `ix86_emit_swsqrtsf`, `-mrecip`, and `TARGET_RECIP_*` in
     `i386-expand.cc` and `i386.cc`.
   - Whether the KNC sequence should be an inline expander in `imci.md`, a
     `define_insn_and_split`, or a libgcc helper with a vector ABI, and the cost the vectorizer
     then sees.
5. **Proof and test plan.**
   - How we show correct rounding.
   - Exhaustive testing over all 2^32 FP32 inputs for `sqrt`: is it feasible on the card at
     80 threads? Estimate it.
   - Exhaustive division is not feasible, even per exponent class. Say which hard-case sets to
     use instead: Kahan's, Markstein's, Tang's, ULP-boundary cases, and subnormal quotients, plus
     random comparison against MPFR.
   - Where MPFR is available on our host. GCC's build links it, so check `.work/_shared/gcc-build`
     and the Cygwin toolchain for `libmpfr`.
   - The card time each test needs, so the lead can book it with the card's other user.
6. **Recommendation.** For FP32 and FP64 separately:
   - implement this sprint, later, or never by default;
   - the gcc tickets that follow;
   - the acceptance tests each one needs.
