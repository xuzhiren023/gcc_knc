Owner, 2026-11-14: S12-07 stopped at parity with GCC 5.1.1 because it only tried turning existing features off; try
the two legal ways to make the sparse loop faster than today, and aim to beat our current code, not to tie the donor.
Loop shape: `for (j = row[i]; j < row[i+1]; j++) sum += val[j] * x[col[j]];` with strict FP (no reassociation).
Inputs: S12-07's reproducer and results (docs/research/s12-07-sparse.md, `.work/_shared/S12-07/`), S12-15 survey
item 01 (indirect prefetch generation) and 05/06/14 (sparse traffic), S12-10/R05 gather costs, HPCG SpMV/SymGS.

Route A, vector products with an ordered sum: gather x[col[j]] (IMCI gather, masked tail), multiply by val[j] 16
lanes at a time, then add the products to `sum` strictly in source order (an in-order fold, not a tree). Legal under
strict FP; it vectorizes the loads and multiplies only. Measure whether the gather plus ordered fold beats the scalar
loop at the row lengths CG and HPCG actually have (CG about 13 nonzeros per row at class A; HPCG 27).
Route B, indirect prefetch: prefetch x[col[j+d]] (and val/col streams as needed) a fixed distance ahead; the index
load must stay in bounds (guard or clamp at the row/array end); pick d from the latency facts (S12-10, knc-costs.md).

Phase 1 (proof, hand-written reproducers, no GCC change): both routes and their combination against current
GCC-KNC and GCC 5.1.1, cache-resident and memory-resident inputs, results bit-identical to the scalar loop; one
lead card run under 20 minutes. Phase 2 for any route that beats current GCC-KNC by at least 5% on the realistic
inputs: implement it in GCC-KNC as a general rule keyed on the loop shape (indexed load feeding a strict reduction),
never on program names; red-then-green T1 tests; T0-T2, ICE sweeps, x86 identity (lead); stage a paired A/B on NPB-CG
and HPCG (S11-28 fixed work) plus two controls at full/3100/5100.

Phase 1 runs on lane corpus2 with the installed prefix (no GCC tree); the lead moves the ticket to a GCC lane for the implementation.
